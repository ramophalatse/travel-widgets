Household decision memo

Funding December

How to pay for the December trip without taking the personal loan, without touching the emergency fund, and without moving the bond past 55.

Ramo & Fez 4 September 2026 Money Metrics, 12 months to Aug 2026

The recommendation

Draw the cruise balance against the bond at 8%, repay it deliberately over 24 months, and freeze exceptional spending until January.

The personal loan costs roughly R97,600 to solve a problem the bond solves for R10,312. Everything after the cruise balance needs no borrowing at all: it already sits inside a discretionary run-rate of R85,084 a month.

The one genuinely urgent action is not a payment. It is a phone call to FNB this week to establish whether the R2.49m offer is a re-advance (days) or a further advance (weeks), because the cruise balance is due around 5 October.

Fixed income is stable at about R166,000 a month. Net of every category, the last twelve months came to minus R38,549, or minus R3,212 a month. You run at break-even.

02

The access bond is not yet the 55 goal — correction

You are both 47. Paid off "by 55" means July 2034 for Fez and September 2034 for Ramo, so roughly 94–96 months from now.

The contracted repayment is R25,846.89, not the R31,000 used in an earlier pass of this memo. On contracted alone, the bond runs to October 2041, about seven years late. The roughly R6,200 a month currently flowing into the access bond brings the total to R32,046.89, which reaches February 2037, still about 31 months late.

To land inside the 94–96 month window, the household needs to be finding roughly R38,200–R38,800 a month all-in, which is R12,400–R13,000 of voluntary overpayment, not R6,200. That gap is about double the current access-bond flow, and it exists independently of this trip. The R37,229 already sitting in the access bond is real progress, but the monthly flow feeding it is roughly half of what the 55 goal actually requires.

None of the draw scenarios below make this gap worse, provided each is repaid deliberately on its own schedule. None of them close it either. That is a separate decision.

The personal loan costs 9.5× more to solve the same problem, and welds a six-year obligation onto a budget with no surplus.

Rate and term are separable. The 8% is what you want. The twenty-year amortisation is what you must refuse. The discipline is the whole product.

The R2.49m home loan offer is a limit, not a plan. Drawing anywhere near it ends the 55 goal outright. Drawing R120,728 and repaying it on a schedule you set does not touch it, as long as the repayment is arranged in the same session as the draw rather than left for later.

Regent is priced over 48 months rather than the 24 used for the cruise: at 24 months it demands R23,191 a month on top of the current bond payment, which pushes B and C to a R60,700–R63,000 peak — against a household that Finding 01 shows running at roughly break-even before any exceptional spend. At 48 months the peak drops to R50,025–R52,286, still a large jump, but one that survives a four-month spending freeze rather than requiring one.

The bond can absorb any of these three without changing its own timeline. What it cannot do is absorb Regent and the current under-funded 55 pace at the same time without a decision about which one gives way.

All three scenarios assume every draw is repaid deliberately, in the same session it is taken, exactly as Step 4 of the plan below requires for the cruise balance. Skip that step for either draw and the numbers in the chart above revert to the "absorbed into the bond" case: roughly nine months lost for the cruise alone, and up to three years lost if Regent is drawn and left to amortise.

The plan

In order. The first two are time-critical; the rest follow from them.

  1. Book the NYC to Miami flights This week

    Use the flexible EWR/JFK/LGA to MIA/FLL search at about R20,340, from cash. Two cautions: it is a Basic Economy fare, so price it all in with checked bags, roughly $40 to $45 per bag per direction, which for four people off a 25-day international trip is real money (likely R6,000 to R12,000). Even at R32,000 all in you are under the R35,000 budget and R27,000 below the pinned flight.

    Confirm the airport pair before you rebook the Blacklane transfer, which is currently set for LaGuardia.

  2. Call FNB and establish which product the R2.49m actually is This week

    A re-advance, accessing capital already repaid within the existing registered bond, can settle in days at no registration cost. A further advance needs new registration, attorney costs and two to six weeks, which will not clear by 5 October. You have about four weeks and a cruise booking at risk, so this is the most time-critical action on the list.

  3. Fund the R120,728 cruise balance from the bond By 5 Oct

    Draw that amount and no more. If the bond cannot move in time, bridge it on the Nedbank credit card at 9.25%, only 1.25% above the bond, and settle the card the moment the re-advance lands. Do not let it sit: that card is already at R149,302 and has drifted upward every month since June.

  4. Raise the bond debit order by R5,460 Same day

    Arrange it in the same session as the draw. This is the step that protects 2034, and the only one that needs actual willpower. Done at the moment of drawing, it is administrative. Left for next month, it does not happen.

  5. Freeze exceptional spending until January Sep – Dec

    The remaining R193,165 of hotels, Airbnb, car and spending money needs no borrowing at all if gifts, home improvement and new family loans pause for four months. The run-rate gives you R255,000 to R340,000 of capacity across that window. In practical terms, the trip is the exceptional budget for September to December.

  6. Leave the emergency fund alone Standing

    Fixed monthly costs are about R108,000, so R60,000 is not one month of cover. Your instinct here was right, and the number supports it more strongly than you probably realised.

On the investment contributions

You framed this as unrelated. It is not, and the answer is better than you expect.

Contributions did not stop because you cannot afford them. They stopped because R85,084 a month of unbudgeted one-offs sits ahead of them in the queue. The fix is sequencing, not affordability: a fixed debit order on payday, ahead of discretionary spend, even at R5,000. The current Investments line is R2,220 a month.

Set it up in the same week as the bond repayment. The mechanism that protects the 55 goal is the same mechanism that restarts contributions, and installing both at once is why this trip can leave you better organised than it found you rather than worse.

Two things to resolve

The cruise figure needs verifying before you draw

The goals tab says R128,808. The itinerary says R113,752 for the cruise plus R9,370 of excursions, which is R123,122. Check the R5,686 difference against the actual NCL invoice, and confirm that 5 October is genuinely the final-payment date, before drawing on the bond.

Regent Splendor is the real threat to 55, not this trip

R556,631 targeted for November 2027, with R43,870 already deposited. The R512,761 remaining is 4.2 times the NCL balance, landing ten months after you return, against a household running near break-even and R176,478 of existing credit card debt. Modelled as Scenario B above, financing it through the bond over 48 months adds R12,518 to the monthly bond payment for four years and pushes the peak combined payment, while it overlaps the cruise repayment, to about R50,025.

Nothing in this plan survives that booking on its current trajectory. It is worth deciding before you draw on the bond, because both decisions draw on the same buffer, and because the buffer is already about R6,700 a month short of the 55 goal before either decision is made.
